# Directions: where the game could grow

Nov 25, 2026 · Bryon and Claude

Ideas, not decisions. Nothing here is agreed or scheduled. This doc collects directions for the game past what `DESIGN.md` already plans: first what could sit on the **surface** round a hole, then **bigger directions** that would bring fresh mechanics and focuses into scope, and last how **first person** could become useful and fun to spend time in. Each one says what it adds, what it builds on in the game today, and what's still open. When one is picked up, it becomes a feature on the board (`/ingest`) and its plan goes in a `PLAN-M*.md`. This doc then keeps a line pointing there.

The test for every idea is the game's first pillar: **proximity creates effects.** The strongest ideas here push that rule somewhere new, not just add content.

## The surface

The game's focus stays underground, where people live. But a real colony would have a lot on the surface: industry, logistics, antennas and scars. None of it is living space or daily life. Today the surface holds the landing pod, landing pad, solar array and rover depot, on three rings of free building spots (#233, "Decided for surface rings" in `DECISIONS.md`). Radiators already pipe heat up, and the cargo elevator drops straight down from it.

### Proximity on the surface

The surface could follow the same rule as the rooms below, so where you put things matters. Today any free spot will do (Bryon, Oct 10: "nothing to connect").

- **Landing pad dust.** Every landing blasts dust over the surface buildings around the pad. Solar arrays near it lose output until they're cleaned (by a crew or a storm's end). The pad wants distance from the arrays, and the arrays want to be near the rim to keep cables short.
- **Radiator fields.** Waste heat from below has to go somewhere. Radiator panels on the surface could keep frost off a greenhouse or an ice mine next to them, but they'd also warm the solar arrays around them and cut their efficiency.
- **Exclusion zones.** A reactor, a blasting site or a fuel depot keeps everything else a few spots away. That's space on the rim you trade for power or fuel.

### Industry that belongs outside

- **Regolith quarry or strip mine.** A second source of rock, silica and metal besides digging. It leaves scars on the land that grow as it works, and it fouls the air near the rim (airlock dust).
- **Ice mine.** Water from buried ice at ice sites, with a rover hauling it to the hole. It pairs with the deposits on the map (`PLAN-M4.md`).
- **Atmosphere processor.** Pulls CO₂ from the air and makes oxygen and methane (rocket fuel). A quiet hum and a plume you can see. It's the surface's link to the air system below.
- **Fuel depot.** Fills landers with that methane, so belt ships pay more or come more often. It needs an exclusion zone.

### Watching the sky

- **Weather mast or dust-storm radar.** Storms are forecast already. A mast could give a longer warning or a sharper one (how bad, how long), so you can bank power, recall rovers and close the shaft dome in time.
- **Comms dish.** A stronger link to Earth unlocks better trade deals and news events, and makes belt-ship rescues easier to reach.
- **Observatory.** A science building that could feed the discovery events or unlock rooms early.

### Logistics you can see

- **Supply cache or bonded yard.** Crates dropped by landers pile up outside until they're hauled down. It's a visible backlog that tells you your cargo elevator can't keep up.
- **Roads.** Rover routes to other holes slowly wear tracks into the ground as they're used, so the busiest trade routes show from the air.
- **Beacon and approach lights.** Make landings safer and glow nicely at dusk.

### Meaning and story

- **Memorial or cemetery.** Where the colony's dead go: a quiet place that gives comfort to the people who visit it. It would sit beside the crypts below.
- **First-landing marker.** Your landing pod, preserved once the colony moves out of it, could become a landmark the tour company points to.
- **Viewing deck or rim lookout.** The one place people go up for the view. It isn't living space, just a trip people take now and then, maybe for celebrations.

### The hole showing on top

- **Spoil heaps.** Digging makes rock, and the rock that isn't used piles up round the rim as the hole gets deeper, so you can read a hole's depth from orbit. Spoil could be put to use as marscrete feedstock, or as berms that shield surface buildings from storms and landing dust.

**Picks:** landing pad dust on solar, and spoil heaps. Both hook into systems the game already has (storms, solar, digging, marscrete), and they make the surface layout a puzzle without turning it into daily life.

**Open:**
- Should surface buildings affect each other (dust, heat, exclusion zones), or should the surface stay a free-placement layer?
- Does the surface need workers who walk out through the entrance's airlock, or do its buildings run themselves as they do now?

## Bigger directions

The game already has, at least on paper, the network, culture drift, ordinances, notables, tourism and the independence arc (`DESIGN.md`). These eight would each add a new *kind* of decision, not just more of the same. They fall into two groups: **deeper systems inside one hole** (1, 2, 3, 7) and **the long arc across the planet** (4, 5, 6, 8).

### 1. Geology as a map

**The idea.** Give each hole site a hidden geology: aquifers, ore veins, unstable strata, a lava tube crossing at floor 14, a hard basalt layer. Surveying becomes a mechanic (core samples, seismic scans, a geologist notable), and the layers show in a cross-section view as you learn them. A cavern you break into is a huge ready-made space you can't get any other way.

**Fresh focus.** Where you dig, and which way you grow, become real choices, not just "deeper is better." Proximity moves into the rock: a room next to an aquifer stays cool and damp, and a floor on a fault shakes.

**Builds on.** The drill already strikes things as it finishes floors (M14: aquifer, ore, silica, lava tube, gas pocket, microfossils), and every hole has hidden depth limits (#390: soft limits, a hard limit and a reason at the bottom). Both are rolled per floor today. This direction would roll the geology once, when the hole is founded, so it can be surveyed and planned around.

**Open:** how much is shown before you dig? Could geology run sideways too (rings 4–6 opening on a vein), or only down?

### 2. Generations and legacy

**The idea.** Colonists are individuals now, with partners, parents and households (#521, #527). Lean into that: family lines, the first Mars-born, names carried on, heirlooms, and children who grow up shaped by the rooms and culture around them. A hole's children pick up its traits, so how you raise this generation decides the next.

**Fresh focus.** You're raising a people, not just a population. It's the most personal direction, and it pairs naturally with culture drift.

**Builds on.** People and families (`PLAN-PEOPLE.md`), generations (`gen`, the third-generation celebration), notables drawn from the people, and kin grief. Schools and mentoring are in the catalog.

**Open:** what do traits pass on through: parents, schools, the rooms a child grows up near, or all three? Does a family line get a view of its own (a family tree on the person card)?

### 3. Factions from proximity

**The idea.** Frostpunk's tension, kept chill. Groups form inside a hole around shared jobs, traits or neighbourhoods (the miners on floors 9–12, the farmers' ring, the elders by the clinic). Each wants things: an ordinance passed, a park near their homes, less noise. A neglected one petitions, protests or strikes; a happy one volunteers and builds pride.

**Fresh focus.** Rooms placed together breed a faction, so proximity turns social. Zoning a hole becomes a political choice as well as a practical one.

**Builds on.** Citizen visits and promises in the admin office, ordinances, the strike chain (`EVENTS.md`), notables (a faction's spokesperson) and per-person homes and jobs. `DESIGN.md` already has factions *between* holes (blocs, in "Cultural drift model"). This is factions *within* one.

**Open:** are factions shown as places on the map of a hole (a tint over their floors), as people, or both? How do they stay chill: can they ever bring a hole down?

### 4. Deep time: the century view

**The idea.** The game spans decades between eras. Rooms age and go out of style, materials wear, old floors become heritage districts, and some get abandoned and reclaimed. Optionally, a jump ahead between eras, with a "what happened" summary.

**Fresh focus.** Renewal and preservation, not only growth. It's a natural late game for a sandbox whose score is population, where most of the hole is already built.

**Builds on.** Room condition and wear, materials and finishes (M15), the afterglow, capped holes with plaques and names (#390), and the story arc's eras.

**Open:** does the game jump ahead, or just run long? Is heritage a bonus you protect (tourism, pride) or a cost (old rooms that can't be rebuilt freely)?

### 5. Terraforming as a slow meta-goal

**The idea.** The planet changes under you over hundreds of years: thicker air, warmer days, open water in the basins, the first lichen. Surface rules loosen as it does: no suits for short trips, then surface farms, then open-air plazas. Holes start opening up, and the shaft dome stops being needed.

**Fresh focus.** The long arc pulls the game from underground toward the surface. It answers the surface question at the largest scale, and gives the network a goal every hole shares.

**Builds on.** The atmosphere and air systems, the shaft dome as a hole's end goal (M12), the surface rings, and the Mars globe with real terrain, which could show the change (frost lines moving, basins filling).

**Open:** does it stay a backdrop that slowly changes rules, or is it something the player builds toward (factories that warm the planet)? It could undercut the underground focus: how much of the surface opens up?

### 6. Off-world trade and the belt

**The idea.** A second economy beyond the network: Phobos and Deimos stations, belt miners and Earth as markets with prices that move. Each hole specializes and exports fuel, food, drilled ice or Mars-made goods, and imports what Mars can't make yet.

**Fresh focus.** What each hole is *for*, beyond surviving. Trade policy (who you sell to, at what price) becomes a decision, and it shapes culture and opinion.

**Builds on.** The landing pad, belt ships (M14's ship in distress), supply drops, the three money eras in `DESIGN.md` (Earth credits, barter, Mars currency) and the independence arc. The surface's fuel depot and comms dish would feed it.

**Open:** is the belt a place on the map, or a market behind a panel? Does it come before or after independence?

### 7. Disasters with recovery

**The idea.** Not crushing, but real: a marsquake that cracks a floor, a blowout that seals a section, a long storm season, an outbreak. The interesting part is the *after*: rebuilding, people moving in with neighbours, morale dipping and then rallying, a memorial afterwards.

**Fresh focus.** Resilience as a design goal. Bulkheads, spare capacity, spacing and a second lift become choices with a payoff you can see when something goes wrong.

**Builds on.** Storms (forecast, cutting solar), room wear and breakdowns, bulkheads (M12), the sealed network and its air, and the hazards `DESIGN.md` plans but the game doesn't have yet (cave-ins, outbreaks, difficulty settings). Every hazard gives warning, as `DESIGN.md` already says.

**Open:** how often, and does the difficulty setting tune them? Can a disaster ever end a hole, or only set it back?

### 8. Megaprojects

**The idea.** Shared work across holes, years in the making: a mass driver, a space elevator anchor, a deep geothermal tap, a cathedral-scale atrium, a canal. Each pulls from the whole network (materials, workers, rovers) and changes what's possible once done.

**Fresh focus.** A network-wide goal that makes holes cooperate, not just trade. The holes whose cultures have drifted apart have to agree to build it.

**Builds on.** The network, rovers and trade routes, culture and opinions, and the depth limits' hard-limit rewards (a geothermal pocket is a natural site for a deep tap).

**Open:** are megaprojects built on the surface, between holes, or in one hole? Does finishing one change the score (population), or open a new era?

### Picks

**Geology as a map (1)** and **factions from proximity (3)** are the most "Downtown Mars." Both take the core rule, that proximity creates effects, and push it somewhere new: geology into the rock, factions into society. Neither needs a new layer of UI to make sense.

**Open:** which way pulls harder: deeper systems inside one hole (1, 2, 3, 7), or the long arc across the planet (4, 5, 6, 8)?

## First person

Today first person is a lovely place to walk round (`GUIDE.md`, "3D view"): walk the tubes, corridors and rooms, take the stairs, grab a hot drink at a kitchenette, see furniture in finer detail, and watch the lights follow the clock. But nothing you do there feeds back into the game, so it's sightseeing. These ideas would give it a job of its own: the place to *understand* the hole, and perhaps a second way to play.

### 1. You feel what the sim measures

The numbers become senses.

- **Noise you hear.** Walking past the smelter gets loud, and a home next to it sounds like it. Corridors go quiet, because they block noise in the sim too. It builds on the neighbor effects and on #193 (rooms you can hear in 3D).
- **Smell and air you see.** A faint haze in rooms with poor air, a whiff of compost near the farms, and dust swirling near the airlock (airlock dust, M12).
- **Comfort shows in the light.** Happy rooms are warm and lived in, with plants, posters and a little mess. Neglected rooms are dim, flickery and grimy (grime and condition are already drawn).

You learn the hole by walking it: that one noisy corridor just *feels* wrong.

### 2. People you can talk to

- Walk up to anyone and press **F**: a short line from them, drawn from their real state ("The walk to the mine's killing me", "Love the new park"). Every colonist is a real person now (#521), so every line is true.
- **Notables** have more to say, and sometimes give you small requests: citizen visits that happen on foot.
- **People notice you:** a nod, a wave, or a crowd at a celebration.

Most city builders tell you how people feel with a bar. Here you ask them. Walkers can already be clicked in 3D to open their person card (#528), so the lines would sit beside that.

### 3. Things you can only do on foot

- **Inspect and fix.** A broken machine sparks. Walk up, press F, and it's flagged first for maintenance (or you fix it yourself, slowly).
- **Place small things by hand.** Plants, posters, benches and lamps that add a little comfort. It's decorating at human scale, while Build stays for rooms (Build is off while you walk).
- **Take photos.** A camera mode whose snapshots go into a colony album, with dates. It also gives the life checks (first harvest, the first Martian adult, the third generation) a picture.

### 4. Discovery and collecting

- **Small finds.** A child's drawing on a wall, a note left in a locker, a crack in the rock where the drill struck microfossils. These come from real events, so each hole collects its own.
- **Graffiti and murals** grow from the hole's culture: an industrial hole tags its walls, and a leisure hole paints murals. It builds on the culture sliders.
- **Achievements on foot.** Walk from the surface to the deepest floor, or have a drink in every galley.

### 5. Rhythm of the day

- Ride the morning rush on the stairs, eat in a crowded canteen at lunch, and walk the empty night corridors with their night lights.
- **Follow someone.** Pick a colonist and walk behind them through their day: home, commute, work, galley, park. It's the best way to see why a layout works or doesn't, and it uses the paths the sim already walks (`sim/paths.ts`).

### 6. Atmosphere

- **The shaft.** Stand at a gallery tube's glass and look up at the sky's disc. Dust storms darken it, and there's the dome once it's built.
- **Footsteps** change with the floor finish (rock, marscrete, metal grating; M15).
- **Echo** in big plazas, and the drill's rumble when it's working below you.

### Picks

**Talking to people (2)** and **follow someone (5).** Both use the individual colonists, both make first person the place to understand the hole, and neither needs much new art. **Sensing the sim (1)** is the natural next one, because it's mostly sound and light laid over data the game already has.

**Open:**
- What's first person for: a place to relax and admire what you've built, or a second way to *play*, with things only doable on foot (3)?
- Should anything done on foot be faster or better than the same thing done from above, so walking pays, or only different?
